How evaporative cooling works

When water evaporates it absorbs heat from the air around it. A cooling pad puts a very large wet surface in the path of incoming air, so that effect does the cooling work.

  1. Water wets the pad

    Water is distributed across the top of the pad and flows down the cellulose flutes, keeping the whole surface wet.

  2. Warm air is drawn through

    Fans pull outside air through the pad. The crossing flute angles create many channels, so the air touches a lot of wet surface.

  3. Water evaporates, heat is absorbed

    Part of the water turns to vapour. The energy for this comes from the air, which leaves cooler.

  4. Cooler air enters the space

    The cooled, more humid air is supplied to the building. Excess water collects in the tank below and is recirculated.

Cross-fluted pad design

Each pad is built from corrugated cellulose sheets stacked with the flutes angled in opposite directions. Water runs down the sheets while air moves through the channels between them. The angled channels increase contact between air and water without blocking airflow.

Benefits

  • Uses far less electricity than refrigeration-based air conditioning, since the main loads are fans and a water pump.
  • Supplies a continuous flow of outside air to the building.
  • Adds moisture to the air, which suits many greenhouse crops.
  • Simple system with few moving parts.

Maintenance and durability

  • Keep water clean and circulating so the whole pad stays evenly wet.
  • Flush or bleed off water periodically to limit mineral build-up.
  • Inspect for algae and debris, and clean as needed.
  • Cooling depends on outdoor humidity: it works best in hot, dry air.

Where Hydrochill pads are used

Evaporative cooling suits spaces that need a steady supply of cooler air and have good ventilation. These are the settings our pads are built for.

Greenhouses

Pad-and-fan systems are a common way to keep greenhouses from overheating in hot weather.

Benefit: lower air temperature with added humidity that many crops tolerate well.

Poultry farms

Poultry houses use cooling pads with tunnel ventilation to bring in cooler air through the house.

Benefit: helps reduce heat stress for birds during hot periods.

Agricultural facilities

Livestock sheds, nurseries and other farm buildings can use pad cooling where airflow is planned in.

Benefit: a low-energy way to improve conditions in working buildings.

Industrial factories

Factory floors with heat from machinery and process equipment need a large volume of fresh, cooler air.

Benefit: cooler air supply for large floor areas, with lower running energy than refrigeration cooling.

Warehouses

Large, high-roofed storage buildings are difficult to air condition conventionally.

Benefit: more comfortable conditions for staff and goods that tolerate humidity.

Commercial buildings

Large commercial spaces can use heavy-duty pads where a high volume of cooled air is needed.

Benefit: fresh-air cooling for big open areas.
